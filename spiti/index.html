Weather Report

Two things dominate the weather on this route: altitude gain and the size of the day–night swing. The circuit is back-loaded for cold. The first four nights are mild; the last four get progressively colder, with the coldest night falling on the second-last day of the trip.

Expect a swing of 15–20 degrees between mid-afternoon and pre-dawn at every stop above 3,000 m. In direct sun at midday you will often be comfortable in a t-shirt. Within an hour of sunset you will want everything you brought.

Caveats on these numbers

  • Sources disagree by several degrees. Long-run climate records for Kaza give mid-September lows near −4.5 °C and month-end lows near −8.4 °C. Live forecasts in early September 2026 have been running 4–6 degrees warmer than that. The figures above lean towards the colder end deliberately. It costs nothing to carry a warm layer you do not use.
  • Late September falls off a cliff. The 22nd to 24th is a different trip from the 16th to 18th. Do not calibrate your packing on how the first three days feel.
  • Wind chill is not in these numbers. At Kunzum, Chandratal and Komic the felt temperature is well below the reading.
  • The Kinnaur leg can be wet. Sarahan, Chitkul and Kalpa still catch rain in mid-September. From Tabo onwards the air turns dry and rain is unlikely.
02

What Travellers and Operators Say

Drawn from trip reports, long-running Spiti travel blogs and tour operators who run this route every season. A few themes come up again and again.

  • September is widely called the sweet spot. The monsoon has passed, skies are at their clearest, the crowds thin out and the photography is the best of the year. The cold nights are the accepted trade-off.
  • Under-packing is the single most common regret. Operators repeat that even in July the nights are cold, and that by September it is properly freezing after dark. Nobody reports regretting the extra jacket.
  • Wind is what people actually complain about, not temperature. The consistent advice is a hood that protects neck, head, ears and face, or a cap and muffler if your jacket has none. At Chandratal the wind comes off the surrounding glaciers and is more or less constant.
  • Layers beat one heavy jacket. Thermal base, fleece middle, windproof shell on top. This handles the swing from a warm afternoon to a freezing night far better than a single thick coat, and it is easier to manage on short walks.
  • Cotton is the wrong material. It traps moisture and makes the cold worse. This applies to the hoodie most people default to.
  • Losar is described everywhere as the coldest place in Spiti. It sits at the foot of Kunzum, and the homestays there have essentially no heating.
  • Chandratal is basic and people underestimate it. No mobile network, no charging, no hot shower. Camps are set back from the lake, which is a protected Ramsar site, so there is a walk involved.
  • Black ice is a late-September hazard. Overnight moisture freezes into an invisible sheet on the road. The standard advice from people who drive this route is to wait for the sun to come up before setting off, particularly in the last week of September.
  • The camps are still in season on your dates. Chandratal camps typically run until around 10–15 October. In 2025 they closed on roughly 11 October after snow at Kunzum. Your 23 September night sits comfortably inside the operating window.
  • Coming up through Kinnaur is the kinder route. Gaining altitude over five days is far easier on the body than entering abruptly from Manali through the Atal Tunnel. Your itinerary does this correctly.
03

What the Trip Will Likely Feel Like

Phase 1 · Days 1–3 · 16–18 Sep

Shimla to Kalpa

Green, forested and humid. This feels like a normal Himalayan hill trip, not a high-altitude one. Sarahan is mild enough that you will privately conclude you have over-packed. Chitkul is the first real jolt: at 3,450 m it is windy, damp and considerably colder than anything before it, and the night there is a preview of what is coming.

The genuine risk in this phase is not the weather but the road. NH-5 through Kinnaur is a chronic landslide zone around Nigulsari and Chaura, with no alternate route into the district. The 2026 monsoon has been severe across Himachal, though the worst-hit districts have been Mandi and Kullu rather than Kinnaur, and the monsoon is expected to weaken.

Phase 2 · Days 4–6 · 19–21 Sep

Tabo to Kaza

The landscape flips. Somewhere past Nako the green disappears and you are in cold desert: brown, ochre and grey, enormous scale, almost no vegetation. The air turns very dry. Expect cracked lips, a dry nose and possibly a mild headache. Drink far more water than feels necessary.

The sun becomes deceptive here. Midday in direct sunlight is warm enough for a t-shirt while the ambient temperature is 12–15 °C, and the UV is strong enough to burn in under an hour. Nights start biting properly from Tabo onwards.

Day 6 is your highest daytime exposure of the trip, with Langza, Hikkim and Komic all above 4,400 m. Some breathlessness on short uphill walks and a poor first night's sleep in Kaza are normal at this altitude and usually pass.

Phase 3 · Days 7–9 · 22–24 Sep

Losar to Chandratal to Manali

The hard part. Losar is bleak, beautiful and genuinely cold, and the homestay will be unheated. Kunzum Top on Day 8 is windy and exposed, with a real chance of the season's first snow on the surrounding peaks.

The Chandratal night is the one everything is packed for. Sub-zero, windy, no electricity, no network, tents rather than walls. Sunset to sunrise is roughly twelve hours in the cold. It is also, by common agreement, the best night of the trip: the stargazing at 4,337 m with no light pollution is the reason people do this route in September rather than July.

Day 9 then drops roughly 3,000 m from Chandratal to Manali in a single day. Manali will feel tropical by comparison and you will be peeling layers off from Koksar onwards.

Two practical realities

  • You will change clothes three or four times a day. This is normal and is the whole reason for layering. Keep the warm layers accessible in the cabin rather than in the boot.
  • Sweat is what makes you cold at night, not the air temperature. Change into dry thermals before bed at Losar and Chandratal even if you do not feel damp.

Keep in the cabin, not the boot

Down jacket, gloves, beanie, buff, thermos. You will want these at Komic and Hikkim on Day 6, at Kunzum Top on Day 8, and at every photo stop above 4,000 m.

Leave behind

  • Insulated snow boots — overkill for September
  • Umbrella — the wind makes it useless; a hood works better
  • Your own sleeping bag, if Sakya Camps confirms theirs is rated to −10 °C

Before you leave: five things to confirm with the operator

  1. 1That Sakya Camps is operating on 23 September, and the temperature rating of their sleeping bags.
  2. 2What “heater charges extra” covers at Losar and Chandratal, so there are no surprises.
  3. 3Whether there are current reports of a washout near Chicham Bridge on the Kaza–Losar stretch (your Day 7).
  4. 4Agreement that you will not start before sunrise on the 23rd and 24th, because of black ice.
  5. 5The fallback plan if Kunzum La closes with early snow after Day 7. Low probability on these dates, but it would mean backtracking the entire Kinnaur route to Shimla and would change your onward travel from Manali.
